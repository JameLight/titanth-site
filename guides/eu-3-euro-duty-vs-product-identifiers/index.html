The €3 duty and the product identifiers are not the same thing

Short answer: They are two separate changes. The EUR 3 per-item duty on low-value consumer imports started on 1 July 2026 and is about money. Product identifiers (merchant code, manufacturer code, barcode) became voluntary on 1 July 2026 and mandatory on 1 November 2026, and are about data on the declaration.

EUR 3 dutyProduct identifiers
What it isA flat customs duty per item on low-value consumer importsThree codes per item on the customs declaration
From when1 July 2026Voluntary 1 July 2026; mandatory 1 November 2026
Who pays / does the workUsually the buyer or the seller via the carrier's duty collectionThe seller supplies the data; the carrier or broker declares it
What breaks if ignoredDuty is collected anyway, often with a carrier handling feeDeclaration can be rejected; parcel held or returned

What a Shopify seller should do about each

  1. Duty: decide whether to show landed cost at checkout (Shopify Markets duties, or your carrier's DDP service) so buyers are not surprised.
  2. Identifiers: make sure every variant shipped to EU consumers has its SKU, the maker's code and, where one exists, the barcode — check your export free.

Sources: Commission customs guidance on the EUR 3 customs duty (PDF); Regulation (EU) 2026/1022. Tax treatment differs by member state and shipment; this is not tax advice.

Questions

Does paying the €3 duty exempt me from product identifiers?

No. The duty and the data requirement are independent. From 1 November 2026 both apply to in-scope consumer parcels.

Is the €3 duty charged per parcel or per item?

The guidance describes it per item of goods in a low-value consignment. Confirm the exact treatment with your carrier, who collects it.
